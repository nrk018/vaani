# Goa

Goa is India's smallest state by area and a former Portuguese colony on the western coast of the Indian peninsula. It is bounded by Maharashtra to the north and Karnataka to the east and south, with the Arabian Sea to the west.

Panaji (also spelled Panjim) is the capital of Goa. Vasco da Gama is the largest city. The state is divided into two districts: North Goa and South Goa.

Portuguese rule lasted from 1510 until 1961, when Goa was annexed by India through Operation Vijay. Goa became a full state of India in 1987. Konkani is the official language. English, Hindi, Marathi, and Portuguese are also widely used.

Goa's coastline, laterite plateaus, and monsoon-green hinterland shape its culture. Basilica of Bom Jesus in Old Goa holds the relics of St. Francis Xavier and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, along with the churches and convents of Old Goa. Hindu temples such as Mangeshi and Shantadurga sit in Ponda taluka.

The state is known for beaches (Calangute, Baga, Palolem, Agonda), spice plantations, feni (a cashew or coconut spirit), seafood, and a music culture that ranges from mando to trance. Tourism is a major part of the economy, alongside mining historically, pharmaceuticals, and a growing builder/startup scene.

Goa's night palette — Arabian-sea teal, laterite red, and warm gold — is the visual language of Vaani.

# Konkani and Goan speech

Konkani (कोंकणी / Konknni) is the official language of Goa. It is an Indo-Aryan language of the Konkan coast, written in Devanagari in official Goa use, and also in Romi (Latin) script among many Catholic Goans. Kannada, Malayalam, and Perso-Arabic scripts appear in other Konkani communities.

A common Konkani greeting is "Dev borem korum" — "May God do good" / a blessing hello. Replies include "Dev borem korum" in return. "Deu boro dis dium" wishes a good day. "Hai" and "hói" are informal yes. "Naka" is no.

Konkani is closely related to Marathi. Speakers often understand Marathi; MSMARCO-XI does not include a Konkani split, so Vaani indexes Marathi as the nearest Indic neighbor and keeps a dedicated Goa knowledge pack for local facts and greetings.

Goan English is a distinct coastal variety. Goan Hindi is widely understood because of tourism and internal migration. Vaani answers in the language the user spoke: English, Hindi, or a Konkani greeting acknowledged in kind.

Vaani (वाणी) means "voice" or "speech" in Sanskrit and many Indic languages. The product is a living voice that only speaks from evidence.
